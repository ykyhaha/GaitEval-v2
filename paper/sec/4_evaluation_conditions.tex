\paragraph{Zero-shot evidence use.}
Summary zero-shot macro-F1 is near stratified chance or below it on several datasets, but performance alone does not establish whether a model used the input. CARE-PD exceeds shuffled evidence by 0.065 macro-F1 (archived participant-cluster 95\% interval $[0.007,0.118]$, paired bootstrap tail area 0.029). This is evidence of clip-specific responsiveness in that tested setting, while the 0.321 score remains far below its 0.659 LR reference. GMFCS's summary advantage over shuffled evidence is 0.052 (tail area 0.089), and reversing candidate order raises macro-F1 to 0.322. The latter sensitivity cautions against equating plausible accuracy with stable evidence grounding.

Table~\ref{v2detail:tab:zero_shot_full} gives the complete five-family results; Table~\ref{v22:tab:evidence_use} adds the six-dataset evidence-use contrasts and uncertainty summaries. The five-family axis supports the accepted finding of near-chance agreement in the four larger-test model-comparison experiments ($\kappa=-0.069$ to 0.043 after the GMFCS split correction), but these results concern the stated test populations. The CARE-PD model-comparison cells have $\kappa=0.099$--0.129; ASD-Kinect ranges from $-0.100$ to 0.200. Thus, neither the original nor expanded evidence justifies a universal claim that zero-shot LLMs cannot use pose-derived evidence. Original overlapping GMFCS cells are retained only as an explicitly labeled before-correction audit. The 866-clip GMFCS results describe the corrected model-comparison experiment; the shared-condition evaluation separately uses 255 clips.

Table~\ref{v2detail:tab:modality_ablation} combines recorded input-view probes rather than a single matched-test ablation. The raw-coordinate row uses Llama-3.3-70B on 150 clips per reported dataset; the other rows use Qwen2.5-7B. GMFCS requires particular care: its summary entry is a log-only 866-clip run, normalized coordinates remain unverified, and both feature entries are historical 899-clip aggregates. These entries are excluded from matched-test comparisons and the following interpretation. The remaining model/view test counts are 20/1,647/665/120/2,181 for ASD-Kinect/ATAXIA/CARE-PD/PARKINSONISM/PATHOGAIT. Input views can change prediction distributions without consistently restoring agreement. For the ATAXIA/PARKINSONISM/PATHOGAIT input-view probes, $\kappa$ remains between $-0.095$ and 0.057. In the shared-condition evaluation, PARKINSONISM's named z-feature view reaches 0.757 macro-F1 and $\kappa=0.520$, versus 0.396 macro-F1 for summaries. Its archived subject-cluster interval is $[0.543,0.906]$, based on only 29 clips from five participants. The point estimate exceeds the matched linear references, but this small test does not establish population-level superiority. CARE-PD shows the opposite representation preference: summary macro-F1 is 0.321 versus 0.133 for z-features.

On ASD-Kinect, summary and z-feature scores are 0.310 and 0.436, both below the matched linear point estimates. ATAXIA's eight clips cannot support a powered model ranking. PATHOGAIT is low under both zero-shot views; GMFCS summaries outperform its z-feature encoding. These six rows jointly show representation dependence rather than a universally best input view.

The same-feature references establish recoverable supervised signal, with LR macro-F1 from 0.561 to 0.881 in the shared-condition evaluation. Reference strength and uncertainty remain dataset-specific: ASD-Kinect LR has a wide interval and an archived permutation value of 0.09. They do not show that supervised and zero-shot learning have equivalent information access. Per-class recall and confusion matrices in the supplement expose concentrated outputs that aggregate accuracy can conceal.

\subsection{Adaptation Recovers Dataset-Specific Mappings}
\label{sec:adaptation}
Summary-SFT changes macro-F1 by 0.000, 0.061, 0.124, 0.202, 0.146, and 0.382 for ASD-Kinect, ATAXIA, CARE-PD, GMFCS, PARKINSONISM, and PATHOGAIT, respectively. GMFCS improves to 0.467; its archived paired participant-bootstrap tail area is 0.001. CARE-PD improves to 0.445, but its corresponding tail area is 0.43. Both remain below matched LR. PARKINSONISM SFT remains below its zero-shot z-feature result. PATHOGAIT improves substantially but remains below its capture-quality-free linear references; two participants prevent a population-level gain claim. ASD-Kinect shows no summary-SFT macro-F1 gain, despite a change in the class receiving most predictions.

The adaptation ladder reports all available supervision settings on their stated test records (Supplementary Table~\ref{supp-v2detail:tab:adaptation_ladder}). Few-shot ATAXIA improves from 0.406 to 0.697 macro-F1. Within the same 7B model, ATAXIA summary agreement rises from $\kappa=0.046$ to 0.507, and feature-SFT reaches 0.762 macro-F1 versus LR 0.763. Its participant comparison does not detect a difference ($p=0.97$); this is not an equivalence test. PATHOGAIT feature-SFT reaches 0.687 versus LR 0.662, but the direction reverses for one of its two participants. These results support in-distribution recovery on the tested populations; they do not establish general diagnostic knowledge.

One PATHOGAIT feature in the 2,181-clip adaptation experiment, valid-frame fraction, carries capture-related information. Removing it reduces LR/SVM macro-F1 from 0.662/0.644 to 0.564/0.560. A recovered feature-SFT rerun also removes that field and reaches 0.632 macro-F1, compared with 0.687 when it is retained. The seven shared conditions exclude capture-quality variables for all six datasets. Their PATHOGAIT test has 1,440 clips, so the 2,181-clip feature-removal check is interpreted on its own matched test IDs. We therefore interpret adaptation as learning from the supplied measurements, without a kinematics-only or population-level superiority claim.

\subsection{Release and Limitations}
\label{sec:release}
Supplementary Table~\ref{supp-supp:tab:coverage_matrix} records completed and missing configurations across the same six benchmark members. The same dataset membership applies to every experiment; model and input coverage are stated explicitly. Missing configurations are not interpreted as zero performance.


The independent project repository provides six-dataset predictions, scoring/plotting code, runner and SFT configurations, table-to-file mappings, checksums, and source-access notes. The correction bundle adds the GMFCS participant maps for the corrected 866-clip experiment and retrained-model predictions, PATHOGAIT feature-removal predictions, and scoring evidence. These assets originate from the authors' released \href{https://github.com/gaiteval/GaitEval}{GaitEval repository} and the source camera-ready archive, with immutable commits recorded in the bundle. They support score and split checks without a new model execution. Source poses, complete shared-condition input layers, adapters, and full training logs are not bundled, so end-to-end retraining still requires authorized external inputs. Dataset and image rights remain separate from the code license. Original RGB recordings are not redistributed; GMFCS source access concerns de-identified trajectories rather than clinical video. The permission ledger distinguishes source datasets, publication illustrations, and newly rendered skeletons. A dataset access link or code license alone is not treated as permission to redistribute recordings or published illustrations.

Small test populations, performed-gait provenance, source simulation uncertainty, unequal class support, and missing training artifacts limit interpretation even when the number of clips is large. The verified 866-clip split repair answers the original GMFCS overlap concern for that experiment; it does not independently certify the 255-clip evaluation's training partition. The two-participant PATHOGAIT ablation is a fixed-split observation, not evidence of population-level superiority. This revision incorporates existing reruns and checks their released outputs; no new model training or inference is claimed. Complete shared-condition inputs and unresolved source-image reuse permissions remain listed in the release checklist.
